# Account and simulation audit — October 1, 2026

Independent read-only review of `research/cycle8_us_only/{core,run,supplement,test_engine}.py`, preregistration, results and account CSV. No original file changed. No new portfolio selection or return recomputation. Source rules were reopened October 1.

The original report's **no fully validated winner** conclusion is warranted. Its unlevered P3 implementation is structurally credible. Externally borrowed fund rows and 50% maintenance sensitivities should not be treated as realizable broker outcomes; several modeled paths begin with forbidden collateral requirements. This audit does not independently certify the reported bootstrap probabilities.

## Material findings

1. **Initial margin constraints are reported after the simulation, not enforced in it.** `core.simulate` establishes the requested target before checking margin at the end of a return step. Every funded instrument gets the same30% requirement, including daily leveraged and stacked ETFs. `supplement.py` subsequently marks eight rows initially infeasible: RSSB@2, RSST@2, GDE@2, P7a@1.5, P7a@2, P7b@1.25, P7b@1.5, P7b@2. The primary results still show paths/call counts for these rows. RESULTS discloses their counterfactual status, which is appropriate, but “all account realism completed” is too strong.

2. **The50% stress produces impossible openings and repeated re-leveraging.** P4@2 initially holds2.4 assets per1 equity, requiring1.2 equity at50%; P6a@2 requires1.0 funded collateral plus0.1 overlay collateral, totaling1.1. Neither can open under the declared stress rule. Yet saved results report500 and519 margin calls respectively. Monthly rebalance repeatedly returns to the forbidden target. These counts are artifacts of an unconstrained target/rebalance algorithm, not credible liquidation histories. A corrected sensitivity should label the target initially infeasible and stop or use an explicitly separate constrained implementation. Do not silently lower target exposure and retain its original label.

3. **CAD-financed overlay translation omits the exchange-rate level.** In `run.hist(cad_finance=True)`, `X=X*(1+fx)` multiplies each USD excess-return increment by only that day's FX change. If the overlay USD notional is fixed at rebalance, CAD P&L should include the cumulative CAD/USD level relative to the rebalance fixing. Example: initial USD notional100, FX1CAD/USD; day1 FX rises10% with zero overlay return; day2 USD overlay earns10% and FX unchanged. Correct day2CAD P&L11; current formula yields10. This affects P6a/P6b CAD-borrowing sensitivities, not the primary USD-funded path converted to CAD wealth. The original code calls it an approximation, but the omission is a concrete mismatch with fixed USD overlay notional.

4. **Synthetic overlay10% collateral is not a verified retail implementation.** P6a/P6b use generic excess-return sleeves, fixed notionals, and a10% overlay requirement. No contract-level futures maintenance, cash collateral schedule, basis, roll, variation margin or actual instrument mapping is implemented. The hypothetical growth calculation is useful; zero calls does not establish a realizable Canadian account. A50%RSST/50%SPY funded mixture has the stated100%equity+50%trend exposure algebra, but RSST is not AQR TSMOM and its cost/collateral dynamics differ. The report correctly calls that an approximation and does not backtest it as an added menu row.

5. **Tax sensitivity is narrow and cannot validate a TFSA winner.** `supplement.py` deducts lagged Shiller dividend yield×15%×equity weight from already-computed monthly returns only for P1/P2/P3/P5 at1x. It does not rerun daily holdings, rebalancing, forward risks, Sharpe screens or managed-futures distribution tax. This is a descriptive equity-dividend sensitivity, exactly as its CSV scope says. It should remain so. RRSP direct-US-equity exemption and TFSA dividend leakage are correct in general; applying15% to total returns or all bond/trend distributions would be wrong. P3's synthetic equity series is a broad-market proxy while dividend yield is Shiller S&P-style: another approximation rather than actual historical SPY tax accounting.

## Lesser implementation limitations

- Liquidation algebra itself preserves the realized funding loss: for scaled positions k, equity decreases by1% of funded assets sold, debt decreases by net proceeds, and remaining collateral reaches the chosen buffer when feasible. Overlay closeout cost is not charged by `margin_call`; only funded sales carry its1% penalty.
- Reported calls count each successful positive-equity maintenance breach. Instant insolvency is recorded as ruin, not also as a call; readers must inspect both fields.
- Re-entry after liquidation at next monthly rebalance is implemented and explicitly disclosed in RESULTS. It does not use outside capital, so it is not a recovery-reset bug. It is more permissive than liquidation-to-cash and may conflict with initial margins as above.
- Entry/exit FX costs are added to monthly returns after daily simulation. Thus daily wealth, collateral, and daily drawdowns omit them, although terminal monthly CAGR includes them. The resulting difference is small under10bp costs, but daily/monthly account paths are not exactly identical.
- Trading-cost deduction estimates orders at pre-cost NAV then resets holdings to post-cost NAV, so charged turnover is not exactly the final order list. Suitable as a bps approximation, not exact broker billing.
- Fully funded `tfsa_exact=True` means financing structure only. It does not verify account permission, trading availability, actual instrument replication, or all taxes. P7 proxy index and historical fee mismatches remain; no margin permission is needed merely because a fully paid ETF has internal leverage.
- Margin rates are declared policy-plus-spread reconstructions, not a historical broker tariff archive. Current IBKR benchmarks differ from central-bank policy. CAD and USD debt must remain separate currencies.

## Rules independently rechecked

[IBKR registered accounts](https://www.interactivebrokers.ca/en/accounts/rsp_tfsa_information.php) require fully paid purchases and prohibit account margin/debits in RRSP and TFSA. [IBKR Canada-resident margin table](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/trading/margin-stocks.php?ex=ca&hm=ca&pm=0&rgt=1&rsk=0&rst=080804010401010801) distinguishes50% ordinary and30% reduced margin, multiplies base requirements for leveraged ETFs (capped100%), and reserves higher house requirements. Applying the same linear factor to stacked ETFs remains a model assumption, not verified security-level collateral.

[BlackRock withholding guide](https://www.blackrock.com/ca/individual/en/literature/brochure/withholding-tax-reference-guide-en-ca.pdf) supports US dividend leakage in TFSA, pension exemption for direct US ETF holdings in RRSP/RRIF, and qualified-interest exemption. Distribution character and any fund-level foreign withholding still matter.

## Effect on prior conclusions

P3@1 uses no debt or overlay, so the main margin/FX-overlay bugs do not directly change its primary paths. Its60%SPY/40%IEF named implementation and weighted0.1167%expense arithmetic are valid as a current structural illustration, subject to data and tax caveats. P6a@1 historical-screen dominance remains a hypothetical overlay result, not an account-certified answer. The report already says neither P6a nor any other row is fully validated. Keep that conclusion; do not upgrade the50% forward-risk P3 finding into a safe drawdown promise. Initial-feasibility enforcement, CAD-overlay translation and taxed path/risk recomputation are required before stronger account-specific rankings.
