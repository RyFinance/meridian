# Beyond-the-menu premia hunt — development only

**POSITIVE: none.** Four candidates were preregistered, frozen and run once in development. None meets the predeclared joint rule: A4-calibrated one-sided `p < 0.0125` (`0.05/4`), positive cost-stressed active mean, and no single year above 40% of cumulative positive active P&L. All holdouts remain locked. These are research results, not trading recommendations or owner-registered findings.

## Methodological limits

- This screen is intentionally **not** an independent audit of nulls. The [bug log](BUG_LOG.md) records two mechanical issues and their performance-blind corrections; neither changed a strategy rule or added a return trial.
- Four tests are fewer than the requested 6–10 aim because the remaining non-overlapping candidates lacked adequate frozen free inputs or duplicated assigned/spent families. The Bonferroni rule uses the **actual** four computed tests, not the number searched.
- The A4 `B=159` calibrated p-value grid has minimum `1/160 = 0.00625`; if eight or more candidates were run, the strict `p < 0.05/K` positive criterion could be impossible at this resolution. That cap was not reached here.
- FOMC calendar-time effective N (2,964.9) is a variance diagnostic for the sparse daily active-return series, **not** 2,965 independent FOMC bets: the frozen schedule has only 153 development events. This materially limits economic replication power; event clustering and regime dependence remain concerns.
- Short interest uses a published, author-derived aggregate and cannot establish exact historical raw-vintage recoverability. The French factor test changes the original 20-factor panel to four archived FF5 long-short factors; it is a fidelity probe, not a tradable implementation. SPY overnight and FOMC assume idealized close/open fills and omit Canadian taxes/FX. These limits prevent a portfolio or broker-action inference even if a development result had passed.
