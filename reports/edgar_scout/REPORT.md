Per the task's hard constraint, the User-Agent (`quant_v7-research/1.0 (non-commercial research)`) never carried a personal email, and no attempt was made to add one to get past the block.

## Survivorship analysis

This is the crux of the whole EDGAR opportunity, and the answer is unambiguous: **no free source in this repo or tested here carries post-delisting prices.**

- **Confirmed directly (this scout)**: yfinance probe (presence/absence only, no returns computed) on 14 delisted/bankrupt/acquired issuers (SVB Financial, First Republic, Bed Bath & Beyond, Celgene, Twitter, Sears Holdings, Lehman Brothers Holdings, Washington Mutual, Rite Aid, Party City, WeWork, Silvergate Capital, Enron, old GM/Motors Liquidation) plus AAPL/MSFT as live controls. **11 of 14 returned zero rows.** The other 3 (SBNY, BBBY, SI) returned *some* data — but checking the first date proves it is a **ticker-recycling false positive**: SBNY's "history" starts 2024-08-15 (17 months after Signature Bank was seized), BBBY's starts 2026-07-17, SI's starts 2025-07-31 — all long after the original companies stopped existing. **0 of 14 have genuine pre-delisting price history.** This is a sharper finding than simple absence: a naive pull would silently splice an unrelated company's prices onto the old CIK/ticker unless the listing start date is checked against the known delisting date.
- **Corroborated by the repo's own prior finding**: `research/data_scout/build_vendor_matrix.py` (2026-09-23) independently found the LSE vault (the project's paid data source) has no candles for SIVB/TWTR/FRC/CELG either.
- **EDGAR itself is fully survivor-free on the *filing* side** for every dataset in scope — a firm that later delists still has its 13F holdings, Form 4s, 8-Ks, and XBRL filings in the historical record. The asymmetry is specifically: EDGAR keeps the event, free price sources drop the outcome.

**Designs naturally resistant to this gap** (recommended over designs that need a long post-event hold):
1. **Short-horizon, react-and-exit designs** on distress-adjacent 8-K items (1.03 bankruptcy, 2.05/2.06 restructuring/impairment, 3.01 delisting notice) and NT 10-K/10-Q — enter at next open, exit within days, *before* the free-price gap actually opens. These are, by construction, the least survivorship-exposed designs in the whole scope even though they are individually the most survivorship-*correlated* event types.
2. **Bias-direction reasoning as a fallback where price data is simply unavailable**: for designs that do need a longer hold (13F changes, XBRL fundamentals, insider Form 3/4/5 — already demonstrated), the missing names are disproportionately future losers/delistings, so the omission should **bias measured returns upward** (the prior fundamentals report found exactly this: the short leg of a issuance/earnings-consistency signal was the one destroyed by survivorship, because "today's S&P 500 members that issued heavily... are the ones that later succeeded"). Any result from these designs should be read as an upper bound, and a same-universe control (as the insider and fundamentals studies already do) is the only partial mitigation available without a paid delisted-price vendor.
3. **XBRL Financial Statement Data Sets partially escapes the CIK-selection half of the bias** (the filing side covers 5,000+ names/quarter vs. 496 in the prior work) even though the price-side gap remains — this is why it ranks above the other fundamentals-adjacent candidates.

## Contamination notes

- **No return-looking occurred.** No relationship between any EDGAR data and subsequent stock returns was computed anywhere in this workload. The only price data touched was a yfinance presence/absence probe on 14 delisted tickers (explicitly permitted), and no return series was ever pulled or computed from it.
- **CIK resolution is imperfect and was caught, not hidden.** Of 14 hand-picked delisted-issuer CIKs (from memory, since the ticker→CIK lookup file and `cgi-bin/browse-edgar` are both blocked here), 1 (Signature Bank) resolved to an unrelated filer (Google Inc.'s old CIK) and was **dropped from the sample** rather than silently kept; a corrected CIK could not be found within budget via full-text search. Washington Mutual's CIK (933136) initially looked wrong too (`name` = "Maverick Merger Sub 2, LLC") but is correct — that CIK's lineage is WaMu → WMI Holdings → Mr. Cooper Group (COOP) → renamed again after its 2025 acquisition by Rocket Companies, a real illustration that "same CIK" does not mean "same company" across decades.
- **The first downloadable 13F quarter (2013q2) is not representative.** It covers only a ~5-week window (20-MAY-2013 to 27-JUN-2013) with 95 filers and 166 submissions — almost certainly the structured-data program's pilot release, not a mature quarter (mature quarters run into the thousands of filers). A second, more typical quarter (2018q1) was attempted 6 times with exponential backoff and consistently refused (403); the ranking instead uses `efts.sec.gov` full-text-search population counts (7,975 13F-HR + 1,691 13F-NT filings in Q1 2024) as the representative-scale figure, clearly labeled as such.
- **Several full-text-search counts are capped, not exact.** Form 144, SC 13G, 424B2, and unfiltered 8-K-all queries for Q1 2024 all hit the search API's 10,000-result ceiling; true quarterly counts are higher. Reported as "≥10,000" throughout, never presented as exact.
- **After-hours/intraday classification uses a fixed UTC−4 (EDT) offset**, not calendar-aware DST — filings near the ET/UTC boundary in winter months (Nov–Mar) could be misclassified by up to an hour. The signal is strong enough (e.g. 87% after-hours for Item 2.02 earnings releases) that this approximation doesn't change any conclusion, but it is not date-exact.
- **www.sec.gov access is genuinely intermittent from this sandbox**, not just User-Agent-sensitive: identical URLs returned 200 on some attempts and 403 (two different error types) on others, and even HEAD requests failed where GETs to the same URL family had just succeeded. This is disclosed rather than worked around (no personal email was added to the User-Agent at any point).

## Top 3 recommended information families

1. **8-K Item 5.02 (officer/director changes)** — cleanest fully-independent pick: never touched by quant_v7, ~12,700 events/yr nationwide, second-precision acceptance timestamps with a confirmed real timing pattern (63% after-hours), and a well-established literature mechanism (unscheduled CEO/CFO departures predict negative drift). *Universe*: all EDGAR 8-K filers (no price-vendor pre-filter needed for the event side); *timing*: next session's open after `acceptanceDateTime`, since most filings land after hours. Needs one implementation step this scout did not do: separating routine retirements from forced/unscheduled departures (Item 5.02 alone doesn't distinguish these) via the filing text.

2. **XBRL Financial Statement Data Sets** — directly answers the prior fundamentals study's own stated blocker: the same issuance/earnings-consistency mechanisms, tested on 5,500+ filers/quarter instead of 496 current S&P 500 names, with per-filing acceptance timestamps available via the submissions API for point-in-time entry. *Universe*: all XBRL filers, not pre-filtered to any index; *timing*: next open after acceptance datetime (found via `sub.txt`'s accession number cross-referenced to `data.sec.gov/submissions`). Caveat: tag/field inconsistency across filers is a real implementation cost, and the free-price gap for delisted names still applies to the *return* side even though the *filing* side is now survivor-complete.

3. **SC 13D activist filings** (13D only — exclude the 13G/13D-A amendment majority, which is 3.5–4.4× the volume of new stakes) — the strongest single economic-mechanism score in this scope (hedge-fund-activism announcement returns are one of the most replicated findings in the literature), ~6,500 genuinely new-stake events/yr, never touched by quant_v7, and a short (10-day) statutory disclosure lag that keeps the point-in-time gap tight. *Universe*: all SC 13D filers on operating companies (exclude passive-only 13G); *timing*: next open after acceptance datetime, holding through the typical multi-month activist campaign window rather than a single-day reaction.

(8-K Item 2.02 scored higher than all three on the strict lexicographic ranking, but it is listed for context rather than as a top-3 pick: its economic mechanism is PEAD, which the project has already examined via a different data pipeline, so it adds timestamp precision and universe breadth rather than a genuinely new hypothesis.)
