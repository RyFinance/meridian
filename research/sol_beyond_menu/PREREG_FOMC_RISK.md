# Preregistration: scheduled FOMC announcement-risk premium

Status: LOCKED BEFORE RETURNS. Research only; not owner-registered or a forward signal.

Source: Savor and Wilson, *How Much Do Investors Care About Macroeconomic Risk? Evidence from Scheduled Economic Announcements*, JFQA 2013; author working-paper PDF p. 2–3 identifies scheduled CPI/PPI, employment, and FOMC days and p. 16 reports the event-day excess-return contrast. https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/0906.pdf ; journal https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/how-much-do-investors-care-about-macroeconomic-risk-evidence-from-scheduled-economic-announcements/C6A48B33065D93550C87FEB53C029A0B . The economic claim is compensation for bearing resolution of scheduled macroeconomic risk, not trading the surprise after it is known. This prereg is the paper's FOMC-only category, a declared reduced-event subset because complete historical BLS release-calendar vintages are not frozen.

Universe: one highly liquid U.S. broad-equity ETF, SPY. Events are the **last day of scheduled FOMC meetings** in the Federal Reserve's historical-year pages, 1994–2019. The event date is fixed by meeting calendar known in advance, not by policy direction or surprise. Conference calls, intermeeting/unscheduled decisions, notation votes and cancelled meetings are excluded mechanically. Official historical-year pages and the extracted schedule are both frozen. Eligible 1994-01 through 2019-12. **Locked holdout: 2013-01-01 through 2019-12-31**, the final seven of 26 years (26.9%). Development 1994-01-01 through 2012-12-31. No 2013+ strategy return is calculated this cycle. The paper's 2013 publication means this run has no post-publication development returns; later externally published tests motivate the candidate but our holdout remains locked.

Position: buy +1 SPY notional at the previous trading day's close, sell at scheduled decision-day close, otherwise hold cash. Event-day raw excess return uses `(Close_t+Dividend_t)/Close_(t-1)-1 - RF_month/n_trading_days_month`, with French archived monthly RF as the free cash proxy. The paper studies daily announcement returns, so this includes the scheduled announcement and no outcome-conditioned trade. On each trading day, `e_t` is 1 for a scheduled decision day and 0 otherwise. A4 active is `e_t*r_excess_t - mean(e)*r_excess_t` over the development window. The static control therefore has the same mean market exposure. All trading-day zero-return observations are retained for calendar-time inference; effective N is not the number of events times assets. At each scheduled event the entry is the prior close and exit the event close; these idealized auction fills are penalized by costs.

Base cost 2 bp per side, or 4 bp per event; stress 4 bp per side, or 8 bp per event. Control is an exposure-matched research comparator, not separately traded, so charge strategy costs only. No leverage. Canadian tax and FX costs are not modeled, so no account-level implementation claim.

Inference: A4 calibrated one-sided AR-sieve/HAC p with B=159, H=1, 0.20 regime bandwidth because monetary-policy regimes change. Also apply the predeclared crash layer of probability 0.2 per year and jump −6 standard deviations (`p_day=0.2/252`), taking the larger calibrated p. Annualization 252, A4 effective N floored. Stress mean must be positive, and max year may contribute at most 40% of positive cumulative active P&L. Positive requires p < 0.05/K for actual total run count K. No parameters or event-class substitutions after development results.
